# Stable HTML/UX Contract

Keep every item below when changing `assets/interview-helper-template.html`.

The generated page must always be:
- one standalone `.html` file;
- directly openable from the local filesystem without a server;
- light theme;
- responsive enough for normal desktop/laptop use: no horizontal overflow at 1280px, wrapping at narrow widths;
- dependency-free and network-free;
- fully functional offline.

It must always provide:
- sticky header, static below 720px so it does not cover a phone screen;
- the candidate and role in the `h1` and the document `<title>`, followed by a `·` separator and the `interviewDate` when the spec sets one and by nothing when it does not;
- a generation stamp in the header meta area, `Generated YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM +HH:MM · skill <version>` (local time with UTC offset, then the generating skill's `metadata.version`), written by the generator;
- header actions in color-coded groups, each with a small uppercase caption: `Save HTML With Comments` alone as the primary accent button; `Expand all sections` / `Collapse all sections` as one neutral segmented control, followed by a violet `Background` button (icon only from 721px to 1599px) that opens the Candidate background overlay; `Clear all` as a separated destructive red button; the timer in its own tinted box. At 1360px and wider the candidate information and all four action groups occupy one grid row: candidate information takes the available left column, and the action groups use their content widths on the right. The subtitle truncates to one line with its full text in a tooltip. At 1024px and wider all four groups share one caption row and one button row, and the line under the timer buttons (end-time action, or `Started at` while it is shown) adds no width to the timer group, so the header never scrolls sideways. The question filter sits in the candidate column under the title and generation stamp; at 1360px and wider the stamp keeps one line with its full text in a tooltip. Below 1360px the title takes its own row above the groups (the title's date never breaks across lines), and the sticky sidebar starts below the taller header;
- sticky sidebar and anchor-scroll offsets track the actual header height on load, resize, and header content changes; static mobile headers contribute zero to that offset;
- the sidebar lists `Interview flow` first, then `Progress`, then `Flagged moments` last because it is the only card that grows during the interview; at 721–1080px it sits above the content: `Interview flow` across the full width as one wrapping row of pills in reading order, then `Progress` and `Flagged moments` side by side;
- inline SVG icons on the header buttons, the done buttons, the collapse chevrons, the rating scales, the overall-signal chips, and the `Generate analysis prompt` / `Copy prompt` buttons: one outline style (24 viewBox drawn at 16px, stroke 2, round caps and joins, `currentColor`), each icon defined once in the page, `aria-hidden="true"`, no icon fonts or external files;
- compact header typography and spacing; at 721–1023px the subtitle is hidden and the timer's caption and controls share a row. The `Pace` text stays hidden; the timer still colors its digits as the interview approaches or exceeds its timebox;
- interview timer with no standing hint text. The first mark set while the timer has never run or was reset starts the timer and shows a toast pointing to `Started at`. While the timer runs and was never stopped, the slot under the timer shows `Started at` with a local `HH:MM` time field: changing it moves the timer start and recomputes the elapsed time of every mark from its stored wall-clock time (marks before the new start lose their elapsed time; a future time is rejected). After a stop the field is hidden. A stopped timer uses the calm ended style and `Start` reads `Resume`;
- LocalStorage autosave for all interactive state;
- a `Show` question filter: `All` (the default), `Open` (neither completed nor skipped), `Marked` (any rating, observed signal, overall signal, or note), and `No verdict` (marked, not skipped, no overall signal), each but `All` with its live count. It hides non-matching questions and every section left without a shown question, shows `No questions match this filter.` with a `Show all` button when nothing matches, and changes no state. Opening a hidden question or section from the sidebar, coverage links, `Still open`, or `Behind` switches the filter to `All`. Candidate background, briefing, and Final assessment stay visible. The choice persists in LocalStorage and Save HTML; unknown or missing values load as `All`;
- `Start` (or `Resume`) is disabled while the timer runs and `Stop` is disabled while it does not. `Stop` (formerly `Pause`) marks the end of the interview: marks made while stopped get no time, and `Resume` continues the same timer; so neither looks active when it would do nothing;
- a compact `Set end time` action below the timer controls. It toggles a floating panel under the timer group, outside the header's flow so the header keeps its height, which Esc or a click outside closes. The panel holds an `Ended at` local date-and-time field, the browser's time-zone name, Apply, and Cancel. Apply stops the timer at that end, recomputes active elapsed time from recorded run intervals, and preserves all notes and mark timestamps. A stopped timer and an entered end share one calm green ended state with no animation; an entered end also offers `Edit end time`; Resume remains an explicit action. Invalid local times, future ends, and ends before available history are rejected without changing the timer;
- a 22px pace rail as the last row of the sticky header, so it stays attached to the header at every width: one segment per section with a numeric timebox, sized by its minutes and labeled with its number, title (truncated, full text in a tooltip) and minutes (hidden when the segment is narrower than 110px). Each segment fills with the share of its window that elapsed time has passed. A section whose questions are all completed or skipped turns green with a ✓; the section that elapsed time falls in gets a 2px olive outline. Every segment border has at least 3:1 contrast. Once the timer has run, a needle at elapsed time carries a tag `mm:ss · Nm left`, or `mm:ss · +Nm over` past the interview duration; the needle uses the warn color in the last 8 minutes and the bad color once over, matching the timer digits. The rail is `role="img"` with an `aria-label` summary (elapsed, minutes left or over, the section by the clock) that updates without announcing. Sections without a numeric timebox get no segment, and the rail is hidden when no section has one. The rail offers no jump action: the interviewer takes questions out of order, so an open earlier section is not a sign of falling behind. The rail refreshes on every timer tick and state change;
- the end-time action sits directly below the timer controls, aligned with the timer's left edge, on one line with the `Started at` field while that field is shown. It uses a clock icon and the same raised pill anatomy as the other actions, with a warm gold tint. Done, Completed, Undo, Skipped, and verdict-required states all keep the shared gradient, inset glint, border, and hover/focus halo; state colors change through the button palette variables;
- timer run intervals and their earlier elapsed prefix persist through pause, resume, reload, start-time correction, and end-time correction. Repeated corrections use the retained history; Resume after an end discards the overrun beyond that end. For an older paused timer with no saved run history, correction requires an explicit elapsed `mm:ss` value and labels that limit. It never guesses the start or previous pauses. An older running timer may use its current known interval and saved prefix, but cannot reconstruct earlier intervals;
- Save HTML includes a frozen timer snapshot and its end correction, history, and provenance. Existing LocalStorage state keeps priority; the snapshot supplies the timer when restoring a backup without local state. The analysis prompt includes an `<interview_timing>` block after a corrected end with the interview length on the timer. A new mark after the entered end gets no time; an ended zero-duration timer does not auto-start;
- a `Candidate background` block above the briefing, folded by a chevron with persisted state, kept out of progress, section activity, and the analysis prompt; with neither candidate insights nor out-of-scope topics, the block, its sidebar link, and its header button are hidden. It holds, in order:
  - candidate insights, only when the spec has `candidateProfile`: the portrait in serif type with a violet left rule; a `Career timeline` when the profile has one, a row per entry with org and role, dates and computed tenure (`4y 3m`), a bar on a shared year axis (solid violet for a role, hatched amber for parallel work), and the scale line under the bar, stacking label over bar below 720px; then one tinted column per non-empty list (`Stands out` gold with a star, `Doesn't add up` red with an alert triangle, `Worth probing` olive with a target);
  - `Out of scope for this round` (neutral, slashed-circle icon), hidden when the spec's list is empty;
  - every list entry in these cards is its own slip with a border in its card's color: insight entries open with a numbered disc and show their text, their `ask` as a quotation behind a colored rule, and their source as a small tinted pill; out-of-scope entries are dashed and open with a cross;
  - an insight `ask` ending in `→ <question index>` shows the quotation without the arrow and the index as a pill beside the source that scrolls to that question; the question's card repeats the entry as the same slip, with its column tag and number, below the follow-ups and cue and above the signals;
- short candidate briefing at the top, hidden when the spec has none, each bullet a checkbox (`tick when said`) that is stamped like a mark (starting a timer that never ran), persisted and re-timed by `Started at`, and kept out of progress, section activity, and the analysis prompt;
- progress indicator;
- left-side section navigation on desktop whose links scroll within the page, so they work where fragment navigation is blocked (a preview served as a `data:` or `srcdoc` document), and that reflects each section's state, live and after reload: untouched as plain text; expanded with any mark as an accent left bar on an accent tint; collapsed with questions still open as muted text; every question done as a pale green row with a check icon and a struck-through title. Folding a section never marks it done. A `Candidate background` link heads the list and a `Final assessment` link closes it, each set apart with an icon, bold text, a colored left edge, and the tint of its block (violet, gold). The `Candidate background` link scrolls to the block like a section link. The header's `Background` button opens a copy of the block, folded or not, in a modal overlay over the current place; Esc, a click outside the overlay, or its close button shut it, and a `→` index pill inside it closes it and scrolls to that question;
- clearly bounded collapsible interview sections;
- clicking anywhere on an interview section, nested question card, Candidate background, or Final assessment header toggles that section through its chevron; links, buttons, inputs, textareas, selects, and labels keep their own actions, and chevrons toggle exactly once; each header toggles only its own card, preserving independent expansion and question completion;
- section display numbers use the optional spec `index`, falling back to `id`; section identity, anchors, and stored state continue using stable IDs after preparation reordering;
- visually distinct question cards;
- on every section card and question card, a keyboard-operable chevron button left of the number badge that folds and opens the card, with `aria-expanded` and a rotated icon for the folded state;
- a three-button icon rating scale on every follow-up bullet and every positive signal, left to right: circle-minus "Poor or wrong", half-filled circle "Shallow / partial", circle-plus "Explained well" (tooltips and `aria-label`s). Unselected buttons are muted outline icons on a neutral pill; the selected one is a filled red, amber, or green pill with a white icon. One click selects, clicking the selected button clears it. Buttons are at least 28px, use `aria-pressed`, and show a visible focus ring. The Positive signals header shows the same icons as a legend. State keeps the values `-`, `~`, `+`;
- after a poor or partial rating, a row of optional multi-select reason chips under that line, one fixed set for every role, in this order: `no specifics`, `buzzwords`, `theory only`, `team, not me`, `needed hints`, `off-topic`, `contradicts CV`, `cut for time`. Reasons saved under the earlier set load mapped: `vague` and `no numbers` become `no specifics`, `ran out of time` becomes `cut for time`, and `wrong` is dropped. The chips are keyboard-operable with `aria-pressed`, hidden for an unrated or well-rated line, and cleared when the rating is cleared or changed to well;
- per-question negative signals as on/off "observed" checkboxes;
- probe rows for follow-ups that name a `listenFor` or `warnIf` signal. Under the follow-up and its rating scale, the row shows the paired positive signal as a green `tick if heard` checkbox and the paired negative signal as a red observed checkbox, each with its mark time. The follow-up keeps its rating, reason chips, and mark time. Paired signals are left out of the signal panels; a panel with remaining signals is titled `Other positive signals` or `Other negative signals`, and a panel with none is hidden. Stored keys stay `followup:<slug>`, `positive:<slug>`, and `negative:<slug>`; a ticked paired positive is stored as `true`, and the prompt shows both paired cues on their follow-up's line. Plain string follow-ups render as before;
- pale green and pale red backgrounds around the positive and negative signal panels, with bordered, high-contrast rating controls. Use system sans-serif for questions, signal text, and utility text; reserve serif type for headings;
- optional free-form notes;
- an overall per-question signal, left to right: `Red flag` (flag), `Concern` (alert triangle), `Adequate` (check), `Strong` (star), and `Neutral / factual` (info) set slightly apart. Unselected chips are neutral with a colored icon; the selected chip is tinted red, amber, blue, green, or grey. Clicking it again clears it. Stored values `strong`, `adequate`, `concern`, `neutral` keep their meaning; `redflag` is a disqualifying observation;
- a timestamp on every mark (follow-up and positive-signal rating, negative-signal toggle, overall signal, reason chips) recording when it was last set: the interview timer's elapsed `mm:ss` once the timer has run, plus local wall-clock `HH:MM`. Each marked control shows a small muted time with both values in its tooltip. Clearing a mark removes its time. Elapsed time is computed from the persisted start and accumulated timestamps, so it survives reload and pause. A mark set while the timer has never run, or after a reset, starts the timer; only when the timer cannot run (no LocalStorage) is the wall clock recorded alone, never a fake `00:00`. A mark set while the timer is paused or has an end time records no time and keeps the time the mark already had, so marks revised after the interview are not stamped with post-interview times. Resetting the timer leaves recorded times unchanged. Times persist in LocalStorage and in Save HTML; state saved without times loads with no time shown;
- a green `Mark done` toggle button with a check icon (tooltip `Done + collapse`) on the right of each question card, repeated at the right end of the `Overall signal` row so a long question does not need a scroll back to its header. Both copies share one state. It marks the question completed and folds it, for rapid interview flow, and it reflects its state with `aria-pressed`. Clicking `Completed` (shown as `Undo` with an undo icon on hover or focus) un-marks the question and expands it; the sidebar, the progress count, the `Show` filter, and the prompt status follow, live and after reload. Section cards keep their marked-count and timebox chips on the right;
- `Done` requires a verdict. Until an overall signal is selected (`Neutral / factual` counts), the button looks disabled with `aria-disabled="true"`, stays focusable, and has the tooltip `Pick an overall signal first`. Clicking it then scrolls to the overall-signal row, focuses its first chip, and highlights the row for about 1 s, without marking the question done. A done question whose overall signal is cleared, or that comes from older state without one, stays done; its `Completed` button then shows an amber outline and the tooltip `No overall signal`;
- a keyboard-operable `Skip` toggle in each question's action group. It requires no verdict. Skip applies only to an incomplete question, folds the card, mutes and strikes through its title, and shows `Skipped` (`Undo skip` on hover or focus). Undo skip clears the skipped state and opens the question. Existing notes, ratings, reasons, and mark times remain intact. Completed questions hide Skip and reject its handler until completion is undone. Skipped questions hide Done and reject its handler until Skip is undone. Pending questions show both actions, and Done retains its verdict check. The same visibility is recomputed after reload, section operations, and backup restoration. Skip itself neither starts the timer nor creates a candidate rating. The skipped state persists in LocalStorage and Save HTML; older state without it loads as unskipped;
- skipped questions stay folded when `Expand all sections` opens other questions; their own chevrons can still open them. Progress reports completed questions against the non-skipped total, with a separate skipped count; when every question is skipped it shows `0/0 completed`, the skipped count, and 0%. Navigation distinguishes all-done (green check), all-skipped (muted dashed row), and mixed done/skipped (muted dashed row), and treats the last two as resolved without a completion check;
- a compact `Skip section` button with a remaining-question count in each section header, styled in the existing olive palette. Once any question is completed, its label is `Skip remaining`. It skips only questions that are neither done nor skipped, folds the section, and changes to a dashed `Undo skip` button. Completed questions, earlier individual skips, section notes, and all evidence remain unchanged. The button is disabled when no eligible question remains and there is no group skip to undo;
- section Undo restores the prior skip and fold states of the questions affected by that action and the section's prior fold state, including after reload or Save HTML. A later individual Skip or Done supersedes the group action for that question, so Undo preserves the later decision. Bulk expansion leaves a section folded while its group skip is active; its own chevron can still open it. Skip and Undo never start the timer;
- the section's actual skipped total appears in a separate `N skipped` chip, hidden at zero and refreshed after group Skip, individual Skip/Undo/Done, reload, and backup restoration. The Undo button carries no number; its tooltip states how many questions that group Undo would restore. The ordinary Skip button's count continues to mean eligible remaining questions. A group Undo count never substitutes for the total skipped count;
- a `Role coverage` panel before the final scorecard when the spec contains `requirements`, plus a compact link under sidebar Progress showing how many requirements have a recorded verdict. The link opens Final assessment and focuses the coverage heading. Each requirement row uses its explicit question links, status icon and text, question index buttons, and a separate skipped count. Index buttons open the linked question and parent section without changing assessment state. Requirements with only section links show `Section links only`; requirements without any link show `Not in this plan`. With no matrix, both panel and sidebar link are hidden;
- a `Still open` list in the Progress card, under the coverage link and in addition to `Interview flow`, when the spec contains `requirements`. It lists every requirement without `Verdict recorded`, with its ID, text, coverage status, and one small square per linked question (green for Strong or Adequate, red for Concern or Red flag, grey for Neutral, gold for other evidence, dashed for skipped, empty when untouched). A requirement with linked questions opens its first question that is neither completed nor skipped, else its first linked question; one with section links only opens that section; one not in the plan is shown muted and inert. With every requirement assessed it reads `Every requirement has a recorded verdict.` It never changes assessment state;
- coverage uses `Verdict recorded` only when at least one directly linked question is completed, not skipped, and has Strong, Adequate, Concern, or Red flag. A negative verdict still counts as assessed. Any other retained rating, completion, note, or signal gives `Partial evidence`; a neutral rating never establishes assessment. Without evidence, all linked questions skipped means `Skipped`, and any remaining question means `Not assessed`. These states are recomputed after every change and load. They never set scores or imply a hiring outcome;
- a filled violet `Flag moment` button in the timer box (its label shortens to `Flag` below 1200px so the groups keep one row at 1024px), with a count badge once flags exist, that records a flagged moment not tied to any question: its time is stamped like a mark (and starts a timer that never ran), it appears in a `Flagged moments` sidebar card with a focused note field for 2-3 words (Enter returns focus to the page) and a delete control, and the card shows a one-line empty state until the first flag. Flags persist in LocalStorage and Save HTML and are re-timed by `Started at` like marks. No keyboard shortcut: single-key shortcuts collide with browser extensions such as Vimium;
- `Expand all sections` and `Collapse all sections` topbar controls that move one level per click: `Expand` opens every collapsed section and leaves question cards alone; once every section is open it expands every question card. `Collapse` folds the expanded question cards inside open sections; once none are left it folds every section card;
- a `Save HTML With Comments` backup that downloads a standalone copy of the page with the current interview state baked into an embedded JSON block; a reopened copy boots from it whenever LocalStorage holds no state for that candidate and role, and LocalStorage keeps priority when it does. In browsers with the File System Access API the first save asks for a location and later saves in the same tab overwrite that file without a dialog; elsewhere every save downloads a dated copy. Once a file has been picked, autosave rewrites it about 4 seconds after each change while the tab stays open; a failed autosave drops the file handle, marks the page unsaved, and says to press Save again. A reload ends autosave until the next manual save;
- a save status under the Save button: a short line (`Not saved to a file yet`, `Unsaved since HH:MM`, `Saved HH:MM`, `Autosaving…`, `Autosaved HH:MM`) and a small dot on the button while there are changes since the last successful save. Once the timer passes the interview duration or the analysis prompt has been generated, unsaved changes make the button pulse (a static ring under reduced motion), and closing or reloading the tab asks for confirmation through the browser's own leave dialog. A Save HTML copy carries its saved state, so it reopens without the unsaved marker;
- saved state from older pages stays readable: a positive signal stored as boolean `true` reads as `+`;
- visible success or failure feedback (toast) for every backup action;
- final scorecard: each select has a unique ID and a visible label associated through `for`; the dimension's score storage key stays unchanged after reload and Save HTML;
- a "Context for the analysis" panel, set apart from the score cards by its own tint and accent left border, holding three optional autosaved fields, each with an icon, a label, and a one-line hint naming where its text goes in the prompt: final notes, previous interviewer feedback, and the transcript, whose text the prompt embeds; the feedback and transcript fields are taller and show a character count. An `Instructions: standard` toggle at the end of the Generate toolbar (`Instructions: edited`, in the warning color, once changed) opens an `Analysis instructions` panel under the toolbar. The panel holds the standing task of the prompt, one instruction per line, blank lines ignored: the built-in lines with the spec's `analysisInstructions` inserted before the output and language lines, shown with a blank line between instructions. The field grows with its text up to 60% of the viewport. Its edits autosave and survive regeneration and Save HTML. `Restore standard`, aligned right under the field, asks for confirmation before it discards edits and is disabled, and looks disabled, while the text is standard; an empty field uses the standard text. Every disabled button is dimmed with a default cursor;
- final generated analysis prompt, with the `Generate analysis prompt` and `Copy prompt` buttons right under the context panel, followed by a gold `Next:` hint to paste the copied prompt into an AI assistant and attach the transcript there when it was not pasted above. Text fields carry no format or language captions;
- copy-to-clipboard for the prompt.

The final notes, previous feedback, transcript, and generated prompt each offer
keyboard-operable `Edit` / `Preview` controls. Edit uses the original textarea;
Preview shows escaped source with common Markdown syntax and XML tags,
attributes, strings, entities, comments, and CDATA highlighted; a tag alone on
its line, such as a prompt section tag, is bold on a tinted chip. It wraps long
lines and scrolls within the field. This is source highlighting, not a full
Markdown document renderer. Pasted HTML and links remain inert text. Switching
views never changes source, character counts, persistence, or copied text.
Generating a prompt opens its Preview; returning to Edit focuses its textarea.
The copy fallback opens Edit before selecting text. Views reset to Edit on load,
and saved HTML clears derived highlights so they cannot override restored text.

The prompt wraps each part (context, previous interviewer feedback, live evidence, interviewer assessment, transcript, task) in its own XML tag, declares every block except the task to be data, builds the task from the `Analysis instructions` lines (numbered, list markers removed, escaped) followed by the automatic data notes, and escapes `&` and `<` in all interpolated text, so Markdown or tag-like text in a transcript or note cannot change the prompt's structure. Without a transcript the prompt asks for one to be attached after it. Pasted previous feedback gets its own block, introduced as another interviewer's opinion rather than evidence from this round; empty feedback adds nothing.

Ratings reach the prompt with their meaning spelled out:
- follow-up: `Follow-up "<text>": − (weak answer)`, `± (partial answer)`, or `+ (strong answer)`;
- positive signal: `Positive signal "<text>": mentioned, explained poorly or wrongly (−)`, `mentioned, shallow (±)`, or `mentioned, explained well (+)`;
- selected reason chips join the parenthesis: `− (weak answer; no specifics, buzzwords)`; only when a `cut for time` chip is selected, the task section tells the analyst that the interviewer cut the answer short and it must not count against the candidate;
- a ticked paired cue joins its follow-up line: `Follow-up "<text>": + (strong answer) @ 12:40; heard: "<listenFor>"; observed: "<warnIf>"`. A follow-up with a ticked cue and no rating reads `not rated` and carries the cue's time. Paired signals do not repeat in the signal blocks;
- negative signal: listed when observed;
- overall signal: its label, with `Red flag` described as a disqualifying observation, and no time.

Every mark line ends with its elapsed interview-timer time, `@ 12:40`. Transcripts count from the recording start and the interviewer starts the timer with the recording, so the prompt carries no wall-clock time; a mark set before the timer ran carries no time. The task section explains the format only when the prompt has a time.

Flagged moments, when any, get their own `<flagged_moments>` block after the live evidence: `- <note> @ 12:40`, with `(no note)` for an empty note, and the task section asks the analyst to find each one in the transcript and say what happened there. Without flags the block and that sentence are absent.

The prompt carries only what the interviewer marked. Unrated follow-ups, unmarked signals, and unmarked questions stay out, and the prompt lists nothing as "not marked". The task section limits the analysis to the marked evidence. A rated follow-up shows its state through the filled rating button; its row gets no frame.

The final generated prompt must include only content the interviewer marked. A question enters the prompt only if at least one of these is true:
- a follow-up or positive signal was rated, or a negative or paired signal was ticked;
- an overall question rating was selected;
- a free-form note was entered.

Skip or Done alone does not add a question. Each included question shows its title, its `purpose` in a `<purpose>` tag, and its `ask` text in an `<ask>` tag, so the prompt reads on its own in a chat that has no other context. The task section then says that asks and follow-ups are the planned wording, which the interviewer often rephrases, so the analyst matches transcript exchanges by meaning and purpose.

The section's "N marked" chip counts its touched questions: the marked ones plus those only skipped or completed.

A section with no marked question and no section note must be omitted entirely. Untouched reserve questions must not appear in the handoff prompt.

A skipped question that keeps marks or a note appears with the status `skipped by interviewer; not assessed
by this question`; its marks remain available as partial evidence. A skipped
question with no marks is omitted. Only when a skipped question appears, the
analysis instructions state that the interviewer set it aside and that the
skipped status is not evidence of candidate failure or assessment.

Requirement coverage stays on the page, in the coverage panel and the `Still open`
list. The prompt carries no coverage block: requirements without marks would
add unmarked content, and the coverage status follows Mark done rather than
the evidence.

`scripts/validate_interview_helper.py` checks the mechanically detectable part of this contract; rerun it after any template change.

When professional sources are available, `Candidate background` must show the
spec's factual portrait and available career data. An empty profile container or
out-of-scope topics alone do not satisfy this requirement. The validator checks
embedded-spec completeness; browser inspection checks the visible rendering.
Report either level separately when browser inspection is unavailable.

Each career row shows its source. Career entries outside the timeline appear in
`Additional experience (outside timeline)` with their source, supplied date wording,
and placement reason; no unknown date becomes `now`. A timeline with additional
entries is labelled `selected entries`. Additional experience stays in Background,
outside progress and the analysis prompt, and survives the existing save/restore
and overlay behavior. Report plan preservation, Background presence, inventory
coverage, and visible rendering separately; `VALID` covers data/static checks only.
